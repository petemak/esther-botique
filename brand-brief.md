# Brand Brief — Esther's Online Boutique

_Project: esther-botique • Prepared 2026-09-06 • Source of truth: `business-context.md`_

---

## 1. Name & tagline

**Recommended name: House of Esther**
The owner asked for branding that references her name (Esther) and appeals to
women without excluding the men's range. "House of Esther" reads as a curated
fashion house — contemporary and a little aspirational, but grounded and serious.
It carries a physical-retail connotation, which supports the stated goal of
opening a flagship boutique within a year.

- Alternative 1: **Esther & Co.** — friendlier, more retail/lifestyle, slightly less "fashion house."
- Alternative 2: **Maison Esther** — most premium, but the French framing may feel distant to a Nairobi mass-professional audience.

**Tagline:** _Style worth the wait — curated, quality, delivered countrywide._

Shorter options for logo lockups and social:
- _Curated style, delivered._
- _Quality you can trust, style you'll return for._

---

## 2. Target audience

Urban professionals in Nairobi and, increasingly, across Kenya — women first,
with a meaningful secondary men's audience — who care about how they present
themselves and are willing to pay for quality that stands apart from what the
local market routinely offers. They discover products through Instagram, TikTok,
WhatsApp groups and word of mouth, and they research online before buying. Early
on they want reassurance: clear sizing, honest product detail, real photos, and
proof that others have been satisfied. Once a first order lands well, they become
repeat, low-friction buyers who expect to be told when new stock arrives and
trust the boutique to choose well on their behalf. They value convenience
(M-Pesa, pay-on-delivery, doorstep delivery) and responsiveness as much as the
products themselves. Price sensitivity is real but secondary to perceived value —
they resent being overpriced, not being charged fairly for good things.

---

## 3. Voice

**Adjectives:** confident, warm, discerning, contemporary.

- **Do:** Speak like a trusted friend with great taste — direct, specific about
  quality and fit, and quietly proud of each piece ("We picked this leather
  because it softens instead of cracking").
- **Don't:** Don't hype, shout, or lean on hollow luxury clichés ("exclusive
  drop", "you deserve it", excessive exclamation) — it undercuts the "serious"
  half of the brief and the trust the audience is buying.

---

## 4. Color palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Rationale |
|------|-------|-----|-----------|
| Primary | Ink Navy | `#1B2A4A` | Signals trust, seriousness and premium retail without being funereal. Reads as unisex, so it holds the men's and women's ranges under one roof. Strong for text, headers, packaging. |
| Primary | Warm Clay | `#C56A4E` | The "fresh and exciting" counterweight. A warm terracotta nods to Nairobi light and East African craft, feels current in fashion right now, and flatters product photography for both genders better than a pink would. |
| Base | Bone | `#F4EEE4` | Soft off-white ground that gives the editorial, breathable, gallery-like space ASOS and Etsy use well. Keeps the palette from feeling corporate. |
| Accent (sparing) | Muted Brass | `#B08A46` | For fine lines, icons, "new arrival" tags and the physical-store signage later. Adds quiet luxury; use at <10% of any layout. |

Keep pure black for legal/functional text only; use Ink Navy everywhere brand
tone matters.

---

## 5. Typography direction

**Heading pairing:** **Fraunces** (display serif) for H1/H2 and campaign
headlines — contemporary, high-contrast, fashion-editorial but not fragile —
paired with **Inter** in all-caps, wide letter-spacing for eyebrows, section
labels, nav and buttons. The serif/grotesque contrast delivers "contemporary and
exciting but serious" in one system.

**Body font:** **Inter** — highly legible on mobile (where most of this traffic
will be), neutral enough to let product imagery lead, and free via Google Fonts,
which keeps the build lean.

Fallback if Fraunces feels too expressive in testing: **Playfair Display**
(headings) with the same Inter body.

---

## 6. Assumptions made

`business-context.md` was provided and is the primary source, so assumptions are
limited to gaps it left open:

- **Brand name.** The file says "if possible… consider my name Esther, otherwise
  starting fresh." I treated a name as needed and proposed one rather than
  leaving it blank.
- **Price positioning.** Assumed upper-mid-range: "quality beyond the local
  market" and "good profit margin without overpricing," which I read as premium
  relative to Nairobi street/market retail but below international luxury.
- **Primary gender skew.** The file names both men and women but twice asks the
  branding to "appeal to women." I treated women as the primary audience and men
  as secondary, and chose a palette/voice that serves both.
- **Geography of the audience.** Assumed Nairobi-first, nationwide second, based
  on "local customers" plus the countrywide M-Pesa and delivery ambition.
- **Language.** Assumed English-first copy (Kenyan English), with room for light
  Sheng/Swahili warmth in social — not specified in the file.
- **Note on spelling in the source:** the file's "snickers" is read as
  "sneakers," and the project folder name "esther-botique" as "boutique." Worth
  confirming the intended spelling of the domain/handle before anything is
  printed.

## Competitor & reference scan

| Site | Borrow | Avoid |
|------|--------|-------|
| **ASOS** (asos.com) — cited in the brief | The size-guide depth, consistent multi-angle product shots on plain ground, clean filter/sort UX, editorial "shop the look" grids. | Catalogue density and endless scroll — a curated boutique should feel edited, not like a warehouse. Don't copy their fast-fashion churn tone. |
| **Etsy** (etsy.com) — cited in the brief | Product storytelling (who made it, materials, why it's good), reviews-forward layout, low-friction checkout, seller responsiveness cues. | Craft-fair visual clutter and inconsistent photography; the brand needs one tight art direction, not many. |
| **Vivo Fashion Group** (vivofashiongroup.com) — Kenyan, closest local peer | Proof that a locally rooted, women-first fashion brand can scale regionally; strong owned-brand voice; confident use of colour on real bodies. | Over-reliance on one body/style story — Esther's range is broader (accessories, watches, perfume, wigs), so the system must flex. |
| **ShopZetu** (shopzetu.com) — Kenyan fashion marketplace | Local-first mechanics: M-Pesa, countrywide delivery, vendor/product breadth, mobile-first design. | Its documented reputation for slow delivery and weak customer service. Make fulfilment reliability and responsive support an explicit, visible brand promise — this is Esther's opening to differentiate. |
| **Glovo / Walmart** — cited in the brief | Glovo: doorstep-delivery clarity and live order tracking. Walmart: ruthless focus on task efficiency and search. | Their utilitarian, low-warmth visual tone — Esther's brand equity is taste and trust, so keep the human, curated feel on top of the efficient plumbing. |

**Net strategic takeaway:** the market has either scale-without-warmth
(marketplaces) or warmth-without-reliability (small boutiques). House of Esther
should occupy the gap: a tightly curated, editorially confident boutique with
marketplace-grade payment and delivery reliability, and it should say so plainly.
